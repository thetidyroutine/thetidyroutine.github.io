A Realistic Cleaning Schedule for People Who Work Full-Time

If you work all day, the idea of a "perfect" cleaning routine can feel unrealistic. Most cleaning schedules assume you are home for hours. This one assumes you have about 15 minutes on weekdays and a little time at the weekend - and it is built to still leave you with a clean home.

The idea: small daily jobs and one short reset

Cleaning takes about the same total time whether you do it in one long Saturday or spread across the week. The difference is how it feels. Short daily jobs keep dirt from building up, so the weekend reset can be an hour instead of a whole day.

1Set your daily minimum (10 minutes)

These jobs happen every day. They keep the house functioning.

  • Make the bed (2 minutes)
  • Dishes in the dishwasher or washed before bed (5 minutes)
  • Wipe the kitchen counters and stove (2 minutes)
  • Put things back where they belong (a quick lap of the main rooms)

Everything else is a bonus. On a hard day, just do these.

2One focus job per weekday (15 minutes)

Give each weekday one room or task, and set a timer.

  • Monday: Bathroom - wipe the sink, mirror and toilet
  • Tuesday: Kitchen - clean the microwave and wipe cabinet fronts
  • Wednesday: Floors - vacuum the main living areas
  • Thursday: Living room - dust surfaces and tidy the sofa and table
  • Friday: Bins and fridge - empty the bins and clear out old food

3The weekend reset (60 minutes)

Pick one day for this and do it with music or a podcast.

  1. Laundry catch-up - start a load of towels or bedding first.
  2. Bathroom deep clean - shower, bathtub and floor.
  3. Floors - vacuum, then mop hard floors.
  4. Change the bed linen.
  5. Plan next week - check the calendar and see if anything affects your cleaning days.

4Use the "one in, one out" rule

Clutter is what makes cleaning slow, because you have to move things to clean under them. Each time something new comes in, let one thing go. For a bigger reset, try the 30-day declutter challenge.

5Make it automatic

Habits stick when they are attached to something you already do:

  • After dinner: wipe the counters
  • Before bed: a five-minute tidy of the living room
  • While the coffee brews: empty the dishwasher

What to do when you fall behind

You will miss days. That is normal. Do not try to catch up on everything. Just pick up the next day's job and keep going. If the home feels out of control, do a one-hour rescue session: bathroom, kitchen, floors, in that order.
