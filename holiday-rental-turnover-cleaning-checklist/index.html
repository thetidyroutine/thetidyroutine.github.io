Holiday Home Cleaning Checklist - A Turnover Routine for Short-Term Rentals

By Mr. Tidy · Cleaning Routines · Nov 22, 2026

If you rent out a holiday cottage, flat or spare room, the cleaning between guests is the job that decides how your reviews read. Guests forgive a lot, but not a hair in the bathroom or a sticky kitchen counter. The trick is a fixed routine: same order, same checklist, every changeover.

This holiday let cleaning checklist works for a single cottage you clean yourself or for a cleaner you brief. It assumes a same-day turnover of two to three hours, and you can shorten it if you have more time.

Before you start: the turnover kit

Keep one caddy or box that never leaves the property, so you never start a changeover hunting for supplies.

  • All-purpose spray, bathroom cleaner, glass cleaner
  • Microfibre cloths in two colours (one for bathroom, one for everything else)
  • Toilet brush, sponge, scrubbing brush for grout
  • Bin bags, a spare toilet roll and dish soap
  • Vacuum, mop and a handheld lint roller

Step 1: Walk-through (5 minutes)

Before cleaning, walk the whole place with your phone.

  • Note and photograph anything broken, missing or left behind
  • Open all windows for fresh air
  • Strip all beds and gather towels and linen into one place
  • Start a first wash load right away so it is drying while you clean

Step 2: Work top to bottom, room by room

Bedrooms (about 20 minutes each)

  • Remake with fresh linen first - it is the most visible job
  • Check under the bed and in drawers for forgotten items
  • Dust surfaces, lamps and headboard
  • Wipe light switches and door handles
  • Leave the vacuuming for the end

Bathroom (about 25 minutes)

  • Spray the shower, toilet and sink and leave for a few minutes
  • Wipe the mirror and taps first so they do not get re-smeared
  • Scrub the toilet, then the shower and tub, then the sink
  • Check the drain for hair
  • Put out clean towels, hand soap and a spare toilet roll
  • Mop last

Kitchen (about 30 minutes)

  • Empty the fridge of guest leftovers and wipe the shelves
  • Run and empty the dishwasher, then put everything away
  • Wipe counters, hob, sink and the microwave inside
  • Check cupboards for anything left out
  • Take the rubbish and recycling out and fit fresh bags
  • Leave a small welcome basics set: salt, oil, tea and dish soap

Living area (about 15 minutes)

  • Plump cushions, fold throws, straighten the sofa
  • Wipe the coffee table and TV remote
  • Dust shelves and window sills
  • Check the guest book or house folder is where it belongs

Step 3: Floors and finishing touches (20 minutes)

  • Vacuum every room, starting furthest from the door
  • Mop the kitchen, bathroom and entry
  • Empty the vacuum if it is more than half full
  • Take a final look from the doorway of each room, the way a guest will see it

Step 4: The check and the photo

  • Smell test: the property should smell neutral, not heavily perfumed
  • Test the lights, heating and hot water if you have time
  • Take a quick photo of each room as proof it was ready
  • Lock up and note anything to restock

Make changeovers faster

Keep two sets of linen per bed. While one set is in the wash, the other is already on the bed. Same-day turnovers become much easier.

Fix a laundry system. If you wash at home, bag the linen by room and label the bags. The laundry day routine shows how to keep a wash cycle moving without piles.

Declutter the property. The fewer decorations and extras there are, the less there is to dust. The 30-day declutter challenge is written for a home, but the same room-by-room logic works for a rental.

Write the checklist down. If you hire a cleaner, a printed list on the door or in the cupboard reduces misunderstandings. A list you tick is also easier to hand over than a long message.

Deep clean once a month

Between guests you do the visible jobs. Once a month, add the ones that guests rarely notice but that build up: inside the oven, fridge seals, shower head, curtains and the tops of wardrobes. The monthly deep cleaning checklist has a full list you can adapt.

Holiday-season changeovers

December and school holidays often mean back-to-back stays. Plan an extra buffer for winter: wet boots and coats mean more floor mopping, and heating means dryer air and more dust. Keep a mat and a boot tray at the door, and put a note in the house folder asking guests to leave wet shoes there.
