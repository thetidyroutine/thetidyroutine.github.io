How to Organize Desk Cables - A 30-Minute Fix for Good

By Mr. Tidy · Organization · 2026-11-01

I'm the kind of person who straightens the pens on my desk, so a tangle of cables behind the monitor bothers me more than it probably should. The good news is that this is one of the fastest organizing jobs in any home. It needs about half an hour, a few inexpensive helpers, and one rule: every cable gets a path.

Here's how to do it, and how to keep it that way.

Before you start: what you're aiming for

A tidy desk setup has three simple zones:

  • On the desk: only the cables you touch every day, like a phone charger.
  • Under the desk: everything else, held up off the floor.
  • On the floor: ideally just one cable, running from the desk to the wall.

Keep that picture in mind. You don't need to hide every wire; you just need to stop them from wandering.

Step 1: Unplug everything and sort it

Yes, everything. Turn off your devices, then unplug and pull out every cable on and around the desk. Take a photo first if you're worried about what goes where.

Now make three piles:

  1. In use: cables connected to something you actually use.
  2. Spares: chargers and cables you might need. Coil them, and store them in one box or drawer.
  3. Mystery cables: if you can't name the device it belongs to, it can probably go. Check your local e-waste drop-off rather than putting it in the trash.

Most people are surprised by how much of the tangle was never connected to anything useful.

Wipe the desk, the back of the monitor and the floor while everything's out. You won't get a better chance.

Step 2: Mount a home for the power strip

The power strip is the heart of the whole setup, and it shouldn't sit on the floor. Fix it to the underside of the desk, or put it in a tray under the desk. This single step removes most of the visible cable mess, because power cables now only have to travel a short way.

If you rent, or don't want to screw into your desk, look for a clamp-on tray, or use strong adhesive strips on a surface you've wiped clean.

Step 3: Route the cables, shortest path first

Plug in one device at a time, working from the largest to the smallest:

  1. Monitor and computer power go to the power strip.
  2. Video and USB cables run behind the monitor stand or along a desk leg.
  3. Speakers, lamps and chargers come last.

For each cable, ask: what's the shortest tidy route from here to there? Excess length gets folded into a neat loop and tucked into the tray rather than left dangling.

Step 4: Bundle what travels together

Cables that follow the same route should be held together, but leave a little slack so you can still move your monitor or keyboard.

  • Reusable hook-and-loop ties are best for anything you might change later.
  • Plastic cable ties are cheap and sturdy for cables that won't move, but cut them carefully when you need to remove them.
  • A flexible cable sleeve turns several cables going down a desk leg into one tidy line.

Step 5: Keep daily cables within reach

The cables you actually plug and unplug, like your phone charger or laptop power, need to stay at hand without sliding off the desk the moment you let go. Small self-adhesive clips on the edge of the desk solve this. It's a small thing, but the cable simply stays where you left it.

A short retractable or winder-style organizer also works well for earphones and spare charging cables in a drawer.

Step 6: Label the plugs

This takes two minutes and saves you from crawling under the desk later. Put a small flag label on each plug at the power strip end: "monitor", "laptop", "lamp". When something misbehaves or you replace a device, you'll know immediately which plug to pull.

No label maker? Bread-clip tags or a folded piece of masking tape with a pen note do the job just as well.

Step 7: Check the last cable to the wall

Ideally just one power cable should now run from the desk to the wall. Secure it along the desk leg and the baseboard so nothing trips over it. If you have a desk in the middle of a room, a floor cable cover keeps it flat and safe.

A quick word on safety: don't overload a power strip, don't plug one power strip into another, and leave some air around power bricks so they don't get hot. Don't run cables under rugs, where they can get damaged without you noticing.

How to keep it tidy

Cables creep back the same way clutter does, one new device at a time. Three small habits stop that:

  • One in, one route. When you add a device, route its cable properly right away, even if it takes two minutes.
  • Spares go in the box. Never leave a spare cable on the desk "for now".
  • Quick check once a season. Add a look under the desk to your monthly deep cleaning checklist and dust it while you're down there.

The same thinking works for the TV corner, the nightstand and the kitchen charging spot. Once you've seen how calm a desk looks with one single cable going to the wall, you'll probably want to do the rest of the house, too. Our 15-minute evening reset keeps the desk clear between bigger tidy-ups.
