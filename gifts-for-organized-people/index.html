12 Practical Gift Ideas for People Who Love an Organized Home

Some people light up at a new drawer divider the way others do at concert tickets. If you're buying for someone like that, the best gift isn't more stuff - it's something that makes their home easier to keep the way they like it.

Here are twelve practical ideas, grouped by budget and room, with a few tips on choosing.

How to pick the right gift

A few quick questions save you from buying something that ends up in a drawer:

  • Which room bothers them most? Kitchen, closet, entryway and desk are the usual suspects. A gift for the room they complain about gets used.
  • Do they rent or own? Renters can't drill holes, so look for freestanding or adhesive options.
  • Do they like to label things or hide things? Label lovers enjoy a label maker; "out of sight" people prefer closed bins and baskets.
  • Measure when you can. Drawer organizers and shelf risers only help if they fit. If you can't measure secretly, choose expandable or adjustable versions.

Small gifts (stocking stuffers)

1. A label maker

The classic gift for an organized person, and for good reason: labeled bins, pantry jars and cables stay organized much longer than unlabeled ones. Look for one that prints on several tape widths.

2. Drawer dividers

3. A good microfiber cloth set

Unexciting? Maybe. But anyone who cleans regularly appreciates a fresh set of quality cloths, especially in different colors for kitchen, bathroom and dusting. Color-coding is a small habit that keeps bathroom cloths out of the kitchen - tie a ribbon around a stack and add a tag explaining the system.

4. Cable organizers

Cable clips and a small cord box tame the mess behind a desk or TV. Great for the person who keeps saying they'll "sort the cables one day". Add a few reusable velcro ties for chargers in bags and drawers - they cost little and get used every day.

6. Airtight pantry containers

A matching set of airtight containers for flour, rice, pasta and snacks keeps food fresh and makes a pantry look instantly tidier. Square containers use shelf space better than round ones. 8. Slim velvet hangers

Matching slim hangers take up much less rail space than chunky plastic or wooden ones and stop clothes slipping off. A set of 50 is a surprisingly welcome gift for anyone fighting a full wardrobe - and it pairs well with a weekend of decluttering clothes.

Bigger gifts

9. A cordless stick vacuum

For the daily-reset person, a cordless stick vacuum is a game changer: it's out of the box and done in five minutes, so small messes never build up. Check the weight and the run time before buying - a lighter model that gets used daily is worth more than a powerful one that stays in the closet. A wall mount that charges it keeps it out of the way.

10. An entryway shoe and coat organizer

If their entryway is where clutter lands, a slim shoe rack or bench with storage gives everything a home the moment they walk in. Measure the space first: in a narrow hallway, a wall-mounted rack or a tall, shallow cabinet works better than a bench. Our entryway organization guide has layouts for small spaces.

11. A rolling utility cart

A slim three-tier cart works in a laundry room, bathroom, craft corner or next to the desk. It's one of the most versatile storage pieces there is, and it moves with them if they change rooms or homes. The narrow versions fit in the gap between a washing machine and the wall - often wasted space.

A note on wrapping

Organized people often notice the details. Reusable fabric wraps, a simple box or a basket that becomes part of the gift (fill a storage basket with the smaller items above) is a nice touch - and leaves less wrapping paper to deal with afterwards.
