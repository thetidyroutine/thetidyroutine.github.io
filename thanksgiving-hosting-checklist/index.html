The Thanksgiving Hosting Checklist - A Calm 2-Week Countdown

Holiday Planning · 2026-10-28

Hosting Thanksgiving is mostly a planning job. The cooking matters, but what makes the day stressful is everything that piles up at the end: the shopping you forgot, the table you never set, the oven that's busy when three dishes need it. Spread those jobs over two weeks and the day itself gets surprisingly calm.

Here's the countdown, from two weeks out to the moment guests arrive.

Two weeks before: decide, don't cook

This week is only decisions. Write them down in one place - a planner page, a note on your phone, anything you'll actually look at.

  • Confirm the guest list. Ask about allergies and diets now, not on the day.
  • Plan the menu. Turkey (or the main), three or four sides, one or two desserts, drinks. Fewer dishes done well beats ten dishes done in a panic.
  • Decide who brings what. Guests like to help. Assign a side, a dessert or the drinks - and write down who said yes.
  • Check your equipment. Roasting pan, meat thermometer, enough plates, glasses and chairs. If something is missing, you still have time to borrow or buy it.
  • Plan the oven. List every dish that needs the oven, at what temperature and for how long. This one list prevents the most common Thanksgiving problem: everything needing the oven at once.

Ten days before: shop the non-perishables

Split the shopping into two trips so neither is overwhelming.

  • Buy everything that keeps: canned goods, baking supplies, spices, broth, drinks, napkins, foil, storage containers.
  • Order or buy a frozen turkey now. A frozen turkey needs about one day in the fridge for every 4-5 pounds to thaw - a 16-pound bird needs 3-4 days. Write the date you need to move it to the fridge on your calendar.
  • Clear space in the fridge and freezer. Use up leftovers this week so there's room for the holiday food.

One week before: clean the rooms guests will use

You don't need to deep clean the whole home. Focus on what guests will see and use:

  • Entryway: clear shoes and bags, make space for coats.
  • Living and dining area: dust, vacuum, clear flat surfaces.
  • Guest bathroom: toilet, sink, mirror, fresh towels and a spare toilet roll where guests can find it.
  • Kitchen: empty the dishwasher and clear the counters - you'll need every inch of them.

Three days before: the fresh shopping and the turkey

  • Move the frozen turkey to the fridge if you haven't already (check your thaw date).
  • Buy fresh vegetables, herbs, dairy and bread.
  • Make anything that keeps: cranberry sauce, pie dough, stock, salad dressing.

Two days before: set up the space

This is the step most hosts skip - and the one that makes the day feel calm.

  • Set the table. Plates, glasses, cutlery, napkins. If the table is set two days early, it's one less thing on the day.
  • Pick serving dishes. Put a sticky note in each one with the name of the dish it's for. You'll see immediately if you're one bowl short.
  • Set up a drinks station away from the kitchen so guests don't gather where you're cooking.
  • Bake pies and make casseroles that can be reheated.

The day before: prep everything you can

  • Chop vegetables and store them in labeled containers.
  • Make the stuffing base, mashed potato or gratin if your recipes allow reheating.
  • Run the dishwasher and empty it before bed - you want to start the day with it empty.
  • Write a simple timeline for tomorrow (see below) and stick it on the fridge.

The day: a simple timeline

Work backwards from the time you want to eat. For a 4 pm dinner with a 14-16 pound turkey:

Time What
10:00 Take the turkey out of the fridge, oven on
10:30 Turkey in the oven
12:00 Set out drinks station and snacks
13:30 Sides that need the oven go in once the turkey is out, or use the stovetop and microwave
14:30 Check the turkey with a thermometer - it's done at 165°F in the thickest part of the thigh
15:00 Turkey out, rest it under foil for 30-45 minutes; oven free for sides
15:30 Gravy, reheat sides, warm the bread
16:00 Carve and serve

Roasting times vary with the bird and the oven - your thermometer is the final answer, not the clock.

Thirty minutes before guests arrive

  1. Empty the bins and put fresh bags in.
  2. Wipe the bathroom sink and check the towels.
  3. Clear the kitchen counters of anything you're not using.
  4. Light a candle or open a window for a few minutes.
  5. Get yourself a drink. You're ready.

After dinner

Load the dishwasher in rounds rather than leaving everything for the end, and accept help when guests offer it. Have containers ready so leftovers go into the fridge within two hours. Tomorrow, give yourself one short session to reset the kitchen and dining room - then you're done.
