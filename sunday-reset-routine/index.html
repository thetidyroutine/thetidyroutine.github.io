The Sunday Reset Routine - 60 Minutes to Start the Week Calm

By Mr. Tidy · Cleaning Routines · Nov 15, 2026

Monday morning feels very different depending on what the house looks like on Sunday night. Dishes in the sink, a full laundry basket and no idea what is for dinner all land on the same day - the one with the least energy. A Sunday reset moves that work to a calm hour, so the week starts from zero instead of from behind.

A reset is not a deep clean. The goal is a home that is easy to live in for the next seven days. Set a timer for 60 minutes and stop when it rings.

Before you start: pick your hour

Choose a fixed time - Sunday afternoon or early evening works for most people - and put it in your calendar like an appointment. Put on music or a podcast. If you live with others, give each person one zone instead of doing everything yourself.

Minutes 0-10: Clear the surfaces

Walk through the house with a laundry basket or a tray and collect everything that does not belong where it is. Do not put things away yet - just gather them. Then clear the four surfaces that attract the most clutter:

  1. The kitchen counter
  2. The dining or coffee table
  3. The entryway shelf or table
  4. Your bedside table

Empty surfaces make a room look tidy even when other things are not perfect.

Minutes 10-25: Put it back and reset each room

Now return everything from your basket to its home, room by room. Keep it to one pass: anything without a home goes in a "decide later" box that you look at once a month. For each room, do only the basics:

  • Kitchen: wipe the counters, empty the dish rack, wipe the stove top, take out the trash.
  • Bathroom: wipe the sink and mirror, swap the hand towel, empty the bin.
  • Living room: fold throws, straighten cushions, return remotes and books.
  • Bedroom: fresh sheets if it is your sheet day, clear the floor.

If you already follow a weekly cleaning schedule, skip the cleaning and only do the reset parts.

Minutes 25-35: Laundry and the week's clothes

Start a load first thing in your hour so it runs while you work. Then:

  • Fold or hang what is clean and put it away, so you do not start Monday with a basket on the sofa.
  • Check what you will wear on workdays and pull out anything that needs ironing or a button.
  • Set out gym clothes or school things for Monday.

Not sure how to keep laundry from piling up? Our laundry day routine gives a day-by-day plan.

Minutes 35-45: Plan the week's meals

You do not need a detailed menu. Write down dinners for the next three or four days and one shopping list. Check the fridge first, so you use up what is already there. Our guide to fridge organization helps you see what you have at a glance.

Minutes 45-55: Look at the week ahead

Open your calendar and write down:

  • Appointments, school events and anything with a deadline
  • The one most important thing for each day
  • Birthdays or gifts you need to buy

Ten minutes of planning replaces a lot of Monday-morning stress. Paper works better than a phone for many people because it stays visible.

Minutes 55-60: The 5-minute finish

Do a last walk through the house. Run the dishwasher, turn off the lights, and put your keys, bag and shoes where you will find them in the morning. If you have an entryway that works, this takes one minute.

Then stop. A reset that always ends on time is one you will repeat.

Your Sunday reset checklist

  • [ ] Clear four surfaces
  • [ ] Put everything back, one pass
  • [ ] Quick reset in kitchen, bathroom, living room, bedroom
  • [ ] Run, fold and put away one load of laundry
  • [ ] Plan 3-4 dinners and a shopping list
  • [ ] Review the calendar for the week
  • [ ] Set out Monday's clothes and bags
  • [ ] Final 5-minute walk-through

What if you only have 20 minutes?

Do minutes 0-10 (surfaces) and 55-60 (finish), plus the calendar. That alone makes Monday noticeably easier. On a busy week, a short reset beats skipping it.

Make it a habit

Link the reset to something you already do on Sunday, such as a coffee or a favorite show afterwards. Reward the hour, not the result. After three or four weeks the order of steps becomes automatic and the hour often shrinks.
