How to Get Organized for Christmas - A Simple 8-Week Plan

By Mr. Tidy · Holiday Planning · Nov 1, 2026

The stressful part of Christmas is rarely any single job. It's that all the jobs land in the same two weeks, on top of everything else. Spread them across eight weeks and each one takes a short session, and you get to actually enjoy December.

If you're starting later than eight weeks out, don't worry: combine the first few weeks and carry on from there.

Before you start: one place for everything

Pick a single home for your Christmas plans: a printed planner, a notebook or one note on your phone. Lists scattered across receipts and message threads are how things get forgotten or bought twice. You'll need four simple lists: people to buy for, cards to send, the menu, and jobs to do.

Week 8: decide what Christmas looks like

This week is only decisions - no shopping yet.

  • Where will you be, and who's coming? Hosting, traveling or both?
  • What's your budget? Set one total now. Our guide to making a Christmas budget takes about half an hour.
  • Which traditions do you love, and which are you only doing out of habit? Drop one or two. Nobody will notice, and you get the time back.
  • Book anything that sells out: travel, time off, a babysitter for party nights.

Week 7: write the gift list

List everyone you're buying for, with a budget and an idea for each one.

  • Ask for wish lists now, while people still have time to think about them.
  • Suggest a Secret Santa for larger groups if you'd like to.
  • Note sizes and anything you've heard them mention.

Stuck for ideas for someone who loves a tidy home? We've collected 12 practical gift ideas.

Week 6: declutter before the new stuff arrives

Christmas brings more things into the house, so make room first.

  • Sort toys with the kids, and donate what's no longer played with before new ones arrive.
  • Clear space in a closet or under a bed to hide gifts.
  • Clear out the fridge and freezer gradually by using what's there.

Our toy organization ideas can help with the first one.

Week 5: shop for gifts and check decorations

  • Buy the first batch of gifts. Tick them off your list and note what you paid.
  • Bring the decoration boxes out. Test the lights and make a list of what's actually missing before you buy more.
  • Set up a wrapping station: paper, tape, scissors, tags and ribbon together in one box.

Week 4: cards, shipping and the main clean

  • Write and address cards in two or three short sessions with a movie on. Update your address list as you go, and keep it with your Christmas things so next year is quicker.
  • Check the last posting dates for anything you're sending abroad, and aim to send a week earlier than the deadline.
  • Start the first of your deep cleaning jobs. Our 4-week holiday cleaning checklist breaks it into one session a week.
  • Finish most of your gift shopping this week if you can.

Week 3: plan the food

  • Write your menu for the main meal, plus breakfasts and easy meals for the days around it.
  • Work out who's bringing what if others are contributing.
  • Make your shopping list in two parts: things that keep (buy now) and fresh things (buy in the last few days).
  • Order anything that needs ordering: turkey or other main, special cheeses, a cake.
  • Check you've got enough plates, glasses, chairs and serving dishes.

Week 2: decorate and wrap

  • Put up the tree and decorations.
  • Wrap gifts as you go, labeling each one, rather than leaving them all for Christmas Eve.
  • Do the shop for non-perishables.
  • Make and freeze anything that can be made ahead: cookies, pie dough, stuffing, sauces.
  • Prepare the guest room if someone's staying: fresh bedding, a clear drawer, towels.

The final week: the last few jobs

This week should feel light, because almost everything is already done.

  • Buy the fresh food two or three days before.
  • Set the table the day before.
  • Do a quick clean of the rooms guests will use, and keep up a 15-minute evening reset so it stays that way.
  • Write a simple timeline for the main meal and stick it on the fridge.
  • Empty the bins and dishwasher on Christmas Eve, and stop. Whatever isn't done by then probably didn't need doing, and nobody at the table will know the difference.

After Christmas: make next year easier

Before you pack everything away, spend twenty minutes helping future you.

  • Write down what worked and what you'd change.
  • Note your gift list and what you spent.
  • Store decorations by room in labeled boxes, and throw out broken lights now rather than next December.
  • Keep the wrapping box together.
