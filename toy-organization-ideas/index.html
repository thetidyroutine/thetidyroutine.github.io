Toy Organization Ideas That Kids Can Actually Keep Up With

By Mr. Tidy · Organization · Oct 22, 2026

The secret to an organized playroom isn't better storage - it's fewer toys out at once, and a system simple enough for a four-year-old. Here's how to set one up.

1. Declutter with your kids (or before they're home)

Remove broken toys, toys with missing pieces and anything outgrown. For younger kids it's often easier to do this while they're out; older kids can help decide what to donate.

2. Rotate toys

Keep about a third of the toys out, and store the rest in boxes. Every few weeks, swap a box. Old toys feel new again, there's less to tidy, and kids actually play longer with fewer choices.

3. Open bins, not lidded boxes

Kids will put toys into an open bin. They won't open a lid, sort, and close it again. Use low, open bins at their height.

4. Picture labels

A photo or drawing of what goes in each bin lets kids who can't read put things away without help.

5. One category per bin

Cars in one bin, blocks in another, dolls in a third. Mixed bins turn into "dump everything out to find one thing".

6. A home for art supplies

Crayons, markers and paper in a carry caddy that comes out to the table and goes back on a shelf.

7. Book display shelves

Front-facing book shelves let kids see the covers - they read more and put books back more easily.

8. A 5-minute tidy song

Pick one song as the tidy-up signal. When it plays, everyone tidies until it ends. It turns a chore into a routine.

9. A "too many toys" rule for gifts

One in, one out after birthdays and holidays. Talk about it before the gifts arrive, not after.

Keep it working

A daily 5-minute toy reset before dinner keeps the living room usable for adults too. Add it to your evening reset routine, and use the 30-day declutter challenge for a bigger sort twice a year.
