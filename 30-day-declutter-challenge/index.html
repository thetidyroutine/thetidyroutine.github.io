The 30-Day Declutter Challenge (One Small Task a Day)

Decluttering · 2026-10-23

Decluttering fails when it's a project. You pull everything out of the closet on a Saturday, get overwhelmed halfway through, and shove it all back in. This challenge does the opposite: one small, specific task a day for 30 days. Most take 10 to 15 minutes.

By the end of the month you'll have touched every room in the house - without ever spending a whole day on it.

Before you start: set up three bags

Keep three bags or boxes by the door for the whole month:

  1. Donate - in good condition, someone else can use it
  2. Sell - worth the effort (be honest about this one)
  3. Recycle / trash - broken, expired or worn out

Drop off the donate bag every weekend so it never becomes clutter itself.

Week 1: kitchen

  1. Clear the kitchen counters - only daily-use items stay out
  2. Toss expired food in the fridge
  3. Sort the junk drawer
  4. Match food containers and lids; recycle the orphans
  5. Toss expired spices (most lose flavor after 2-3 years)
  6. Clear the bathroom counter
  7. Toss old makeup and toiletries

Week 2: bathroom, bedroom and closet

  1. Sort the medicine cabinet - return expired medicine to a pharmacy
  2. Declutter towels and linen - keep two sets per bed and per person
  3. Clear your nightstand
  4. Remove 10 clothes you don't wear
  5. Sort socks and underwear
  6. Declutter shoes
  7. Clear the entryway

Week 3: paper and living spaces

  1. Sort the mail pile
  2. Shred old papers (check how long you need to keep tax documents where you live)
  3. Declutter books and magazines
  4. Clear the coffee table
  5. Sort cables and chargers - label the keepers
  6. Declutter kids' toys or hobby supplies
  7. Clean out your handbag or backpack

Week 4: the hidden spots

  1. Clean out the car
  2. Declutter under the kitchen sink
  3. Sort cleaning supplies
  4. Clear the fridge door
  5. Declutter mugs and glasses
  6. Delete 100 photos from your phone
  7. Unsubscribe from 10 email lists
  8. Fill one final donation bag
  9. Drop off all donations - and celebrate

Rules that make it easy

Only the task of the day. If you have energy left, stop anyway. Ending on a high makes tomorrow easier.

Decide fast. Ask: "Have I used this in the last year? Would I buy it again today?" Two "no" answers mean it goes.

No organizing products until the end. Buying bins before decluttering just gives clutter a nicer home. Once you know what's left, then buy storage.

Missed a day? Do two tomorrow, or simply extend to 31 days. Nobody is checking.

What to do with everything

  • Clothes and household items: charity shops, local "buy nothing" groups, or a neighborhood swap
  • Electronics and cables: most electronics stores take old devices for recycling
  • Books: little free libraries, schools or second-hand shops
  • Medicine: return to a pharmacy - never flush it

Keep it clutter-free afterwards

Use the one in, one out rule: every new piece of clothing, mug or gadget means one old one leaves. And repeat the challenge every six months - the second round goes much faster.
