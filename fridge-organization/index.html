How to Organize Your Fridge (and Waste Less Food)

An organized fridge isn't about looking good on Instagram. It saves real money: when you can see everything, you use it before it goes bad. A typical household throws away a significant share of the food it buys, and much of that is forgotten at the back of the fridge.

Here's a simple system based on where food keeps best and how often you reach for it.

Step 1: empty, check dates, clean

Take everything out, throw away anything expired or questionable, and wipe the shelves with warm water and a little dish soap. A paste of baking soda and water lifts sticky spills. Do this right before your weekly shop, when the fridge is emptiest.

Step 2: know your fridge's temperature zones

Fridges are not the same temperature everywhere:

  • Door - the warmest part. Condiments, sauces, juice, water. Not milk or eggs.
  • Upper shelves - steady temperature. Leftovers, ready-to-eat food, drinks, herbs in a glass of water.
  • Lower shelves - the coldest. Raw meat and fish on the very bottom shelf, in a container, so nothing can drip onto other food. Milk and dairy just above.
  • Crisper drawers - humidity controlled. Vegetables in one, fruit in the other (many fruits release ethylene, which makes vegetables spoil faster).

Step 3: group food in clear bins

Bins turn a fridge from a pile into a set of drawers. Group by type: dairy, snacks, leftovers, drinks, "eat first". Pull out a bin, grab what you need, slide it back.

Step 4: create an "eat first" bin

This is the habit that saves the most money. One bin at eye level for anything that needs to be used in the next two or three days: open yogurt, half an onion, leftovers. Everyone in the house checks it first.

Step 5: use a turntable for jars

A turntable on one shelf makes sauces and jars spin into view instead of hiding behind each other.

Step 6: stack cans and drinks

A can dispenser or a stackable can rack stores drinks lying down and rolls the next one forward, freeing a whole shelf.

Step 7: keep produce fresh longer

  • Store herbs like flowers, in a jar of water
  • Wrap celery and broccoli loosely to keep moisture in
  • Keep berries unwashed until you eat them
  • Line crisper drawers with a paper towel to absorb excess moisture

The weekly habit that keeps it working

Every Sunday, before shopping: take out anything old, wipe one shelf, and move things that need eating into the "eat first" bin. It takes five minutes and it's built into the Sunday "plan & reset" day on the weekly cleaning schedule.
