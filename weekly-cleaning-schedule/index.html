If your cleaning strategy is "wait until it's bad, then lose a Saturday", you're not alone. The problem isn't motivation - it's that "clean the house" is too big to start. A weekly cleaning schedule fixes that by giving every day one small focus. You do 20 minutes, tick the box, and stop.

Here is the exact schedule, why it works, and how to adapt it to your home.

The schedule at a glance

Day Focus Time
Monday Kitchen 20 min
Tuesday Bathrooms 20 min
Wednesday Floors 20 min
Thursday Dusting & touch points 15 min
Friday Bedrooms & sheets 20 min
Saturday Laundry & one small declutter 30 min
Sunday Plan & reset 15 min

That's roughly two and a half hours a week, spread so thin you barely notice it.

Monday: kitchen

Start the week where the mess grows fastest. Wipe the counters and backsplash, clean the stovetop, wipe out the microwave (a bowl of water with lemon, microwaved for three minutes, loosens everything), wipe the fridge handles and take out the recycling.

Tuesday: bathrooms

Scrub the toilet, clean the sink and mirror, wipe down the shower, swap the towels and empty the bins. A squeegee kept in the shower and used after every shower makes this day dramatically shorter.

Wednesday: floors

Vacuum the high-traffic areas - entry, hallway, kitchen and living room - then mop the kitchen and bathroom. You don't need to move furniture every week; that's what the monthly deep clean is for.

Thursday: dusting and touch points

Dust from top to bottom (shelves first, floors last), then wipe the things everyone touches: light switches, door handles, remote controls and screens. A good microfiber cloth does most of this dry, with no spray at all.

Friday: bedrooms

Change the sheets, clear the nightstands, put away the "chair clothes" and vacuum the bedroom floors. Clean sheets on a Friday night is a small luxury that makes this the easiest day to stick to.

Saturday: laundry and one small declutter

Wash and fold the towels, water the plants, restock anything you ran out of, and declutter one drawer or shelf. Just one. Small declutters every week stop the big piles from forming.

Sunday: plan and reset

Plan the week's meals, clear out the fridge before you shop, check the calendar and do a 10-minute tidy. Starting Monday with a clear fridge and a plan makes the whole week calmer.

Why one focus per day works

  • It's small enough to start. Twenty minutes is less than one episode of a show.
  • Nothing gets more than a week dirty. That keeps every job quick.
  • You always know what to do. No decisions, no guilt about the rest of the house.
  • Missing a day doesn't break it. Skip Wednesday and you'll catch the floors next week.

How to adapt it to your home

Small apartment? Combine Wednesday and Thursday into one day and take Thursday off.

Kids or pets? Add a 5-minute floor sweep to every evening, and move sheets to the weekend when there's more time.

Two people? Split the week: one person takes Monday to Wednesday, the other Thursday to Saturday, and do Sunday together.

Working long days? Move the heavier days (bathrooms, floors) to the days you're home earliest.

Make it stick

Print the schedule and put it on the fridge in a clear sleeve. Tick each day with a whiteboard marker and wipe it clean on Sunday. Seeing a full week of ticks is surprisingly motivating.

Pair it with the 15-minute evening reset and your home will look "guest ready" most days of the week.
